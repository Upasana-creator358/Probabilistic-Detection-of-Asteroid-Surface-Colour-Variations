## 2. Databases and Surveys

### LSST / Rubin Observatory

**Website:**  
https://www.lsst.org/

**LSST** stands for **Legacy Survey of Space and Time**.

It repeatedly observes the sky and records the position and brightness of moving objects, including asteroids.

---

### Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF)

**Website:**  
https://www.ztf.caltech.edu/

**ZTF** stands for **Zwicky Transient Facility**.

ZTF repeatedly observes the sky and can detect asteroids, measure their positions and brightness, and build light curves.

- Uses **g, r, and i filters**
- Described in the project notes as shallower than LSST
- Located at **Palomar Observatory, California, USA**
- Uses the **1.2 m Samuel Oschin Telescope**
- Surveys mainly the **Northern sky**

---

### Minor Planet Center (MPC)

**Website:**  
https://www.minorplanetcenter.net/

The **Minor Planet Center (MPC)** is the official global organization/database for observations of small Solar System bodies such as:

- Asteroids
- Meteoroids
- Comets

---

### ATLAS

**Website:**  
https://atlas.fallingstar.com/

**ATLAS** stands for **Asteroid Terrestrial-impact Last Alert System**.

It is a NASA-funded telescope survey designed to detect asteroids and other objects that could potentially come close to or impact Earth, with an emphasis on providing early warning.

---

## 3. LSST / Rubin Observatory vs. ZTF

Both **ZTF** and **LSST/Rubin** repeatedly observe the sky and can:

- Detect asteroids
- Measure their positions
- Measure their brightness
- Build light curves

The project notes distinguish them primarily by the filters they use and their survey characteristics.

### ZTF

- Uses **g/r/i filters**
- Shallower survey
- Located at **Palomar Observatory, California, USA**
- Uses the **1.2 m Samuel Oschin Telescope**
- Surveys mainly the **Northern sky**

### Rubin Observatory / LSST

- Located at **Cerro Pachón, Chile**
- Uses an **8.4 m telescope**
- Surveys mainly the **Southern sky**
- Uses multiple filters as part of its survey
